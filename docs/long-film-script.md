# THE SHEEP IN THE BOX — Feature Script (draft 1, about 60 min)

The tone is warm and funny, with a little danger. The positive message is that **you become whole by caring for others**.
Dialogue is short, because the pictures carry the story. Lines are in English, and the Chinese subtitles come later.

**Characters**
- **SHEEP**: our hero. Drawn in a hurry, so it is unfinished: a gap in its back wool, one hind leg that is only pencil, one ear tip left open. It is curious, always hungry, and brave when it has to be.
- **THE BOY**: the little prince. Golden scarf, serious eyes, laughs like bells.
- **THE PILOT**: a grown-up who is busy, kind underneath, and tired. Mostly seen as his hands and a voice, with his full face shown once at the end.
- **THE ERASED**: three rubbed-out sheep drawings that still float around faintly in pencil. They are funny company and the hero's fear.
  - **SNIFFLE**: the sickly one. Coughs, wraps itself in a scarf of pencil lines, and is dramatic about everything.
  - **HORNS**: the ram. Proud and loud, and secretly the softest of the three.
  - **OLD WOOL**: the old one. Slow and sleepy, and says wise things by accident.
- **THE ROSE**: proud and fragile, and coughs on purpose to get attention.
- **THE KING**, **THE BUSINESSMAN**, **THE LAMPLIGHTER**: the people of the little planets.
- **BAOBABS**: they start as sprouts that wriggle like naughty puppies and grow into huge trees with roots that can split a planet.

---

# ACT ONE — BEING DRAWN (0:00–15:00)

## SEQ 1 · THE CRASH (0:00–4:00)
**EXT. SAHARA, NIGHT.** A sky full of stars. A small silver plane hums across it, and then the engine coughs.
- The plane spins down in long, graceful spirals and lands hard on a dune. Sand hisses. Silence.
- The **PILOT** (we see his hands and his goggles) crawls out, checks the engine and sighs.

PILOT (to himself): Eight days of water. And a thousand miles of sand in every direction.

- He lies on the sand and looks up. The stars are enormous. One of them **winks**. He doesn't notice. We do.
- Time lapse: the stars wheel overhead and dawn comes, pink and gold.
- A small shadow falls across the pilot's face.

BOY: If you please… draw me a sheep.

- The pilot jolts up. The **BOY** stands on the dune with his scarf blowing, completely calm.

PILOT: A… what?
BOY: A sheep. Please.

- The pilot looks around at the endless desert, then shrugs and pulls out his notebook and pencil.

## SEQ 2 · THREE WRONG SHEEP (4:00–9:00)
**EXT. DESERT, BY THE PLANE. MORNING.** We go **into the notebook page**: paper texture fills the screen and the pencil is a giant.

- Sheep #1 is drawn. It comes alive, sneezes, shivers, and pulls a pencil scarf around its neck. This is **SNIFFLE**.

BOY: No. That one is already very sick.

- The eraser comes. Sniffle squeaks: "Wait, it's just allergies!" Rub, rub. It fades to a faint grey ghost, still there, still sniffling.
- Sheep #2 is drawn. It comes alive and snorts. Big curled horns. This is **HORNS**: "HA! Behold!"

BOY: That's not a sheep. That's a ram. It has horns.

- HORNS: "These are DECORATIVE!" Rub, rub. It becomes a ghost, offended.
- Sheep #3 is drawn. It comes alive, very slowly, yawning. This is **OLD WOOL**: "Mm. Is it lunch?"

BOY: That one's too old. I want a sheep that will live a long time.

- OLD WOOL: "Fair." It lets itself be erased politely.
- The pilot is fed up. He glances at his broken engine, then scribbles fast: a body, a head, legs. **Our SHEEP** appears. It's cute, but the pencil stops halfway: a gap in the back, one leg just a pencil line, one ear open.
- Our sheep blinks and looks at its hooves. "Oh! Hello, hooves." It looks back at its unfinished side.

SHEEP: Wait… I'm not finished yet!

- Too late. The pencil draws a **box** around it, with three air holes.

PILOT: That's the box. The sheep you want is inside.

- A pause. Then the boy's face lights up.

BOY: That is *exactly* the way I wanted it!

## SEQ 3 · INSIDE THE BOX (9:00–15:00)
**INT. THE BOX.** We push in through the middle air hole into a warm cardboard world. Three beams of sunlight slant through the holes, with dust dancing in them.
- The sheep's lines are re-drawn by the light. It looks around. "It's dark. No… it's warm."
- The three ghosts **drift through the wall**, since erased drawings can do that.

HORNS: Well, well. A NEW one.
SNIFFLE: Look at its back. There's a hole! *cough* Is it contagious?
OLD WOOL: Mm. Half a sheep. Seen it before.
SHEEP: I'm not half! I'm… mostly!

- The comedy beat: the ghosts explain that drawings the boy doesn't want get rubbed out. "One day you're a sheep… *rub rub*… the next day you're us."
- The sheep is scared and backs into a corner.
- A **giant eye** appears at the air hole: the boy, looking in. The ghosts scatter. The eye is kind and blinks slowly.

BOY (outside, softly): You're perfect. Good night, little sheep.

- The sheep stands up straight in the beam of light.

SHEEP: He wanted *me*. Then I'll be a very good sheep. And one day… I'll be whole.

- The ghosts look at each other. Horns rolls its eyes, but it is a little moved.
- The camera pulls back out of the box, out of the notebook, up to the sky.
- **MAIN TITLE: The Sheep in the Box.**

# ACT TWO — THE ROAD TO HIS STAR (15:00–45:00)

## SEQ 4 · NIGHT IN THE DESERT (15:00–21:00)
**EXT. DESERT. NIGHT.** The boy sleeps against the plane, with the box beside him.
- The sheep peeks out and climbs out. The desert is huge and blue. "So this is outside…"
- Its tummy growls. It is **hungry** for the first time, and confused: "Grrr? Oh. That's me."
- There's only sand. Then a tiny three-petal **desert flower**, alone, glowing faintly.

SHEEP: Food! A flower is food… isn't it?

- It leans in. The flower trembles. The ghosts appear: "Go on, sheep eat flowers. That's what sheep DO." The sheep stops.

SHEEP: It's shaking. It's small… and alone. Like me.

- A **sandstorm** rolls in like a wall. The sheep lies down between the storm and the flower and holds on all night as sand roars past. Big storm animation.
- At dawn the storm is gone, and the flower is safe and opening. A **golden line traces the sheep's open ear and closes it**. The ear is whole.
- The sheep doesn't notice, but the ghosts do. Old Wool: "Mm. Well, look at that."

## SEQ 5 · THE BOY'S STORIES (21:00–26:00)
**EXT. BY THE PLANE. DAY.** The pilot is under the engine (we see hands, tools, sighs). The boy sits with the box on his lap and talks.
- His stories **appear as moving pencil drawings in the air**:
  - his **tiny planet**, so small he moves his chair to watch 44 sunsets in a day;
  - his **ROSE**, proud and lovely, who said she had four thorns to fight tigers;
  - the **BAOBABS**, which you must pull up every morning while they're small, or their roots will crack the whole planet.

BOY: Sheep eat little bushes, don't they? Then your sheep can eat my baobabs!

- Inside the box, the sheep sits up proudly. "So that's my job."
- PILOT (from under the plane): "And what if the sheep eats your rose?"
- The boy goes quiet. Worried. The sheep sees his face.

SHEEP (to itself): I would never. Never ever.

## SEQ 6 · THE BIRDS (26:00–31:00)
**EXT. DESERT. EVENING.** A great flock of **wild birds** is migrating overhead.
- The boy whistles. He ties long strings to the birds, very carefully and gently. The pilot watches, amazed.

PILOT: You're leaving?
BOY: My rose needs me. And she needs a sheep.

- The pilot hesitates, then smiles, and draws a little **muzzle** for the sheep in his notebook, "just in case". The boy tucks the page into the box.
- Lift-off: the birds rise, the boy holds the strings, the box hangs from his arm, and the sheep peeks out as the desert falls away.
- The pilot's hands wave from far below and get smaller. A big, soaring music moment over clouds and the edge of the sky. Stars.

## SEQ 7 · THE LITTLE PLANETS (31:00–40:00)
Three short adventures, about three minutes each, with a different look and colour on each planet.

**7a · THE KING (purple planet, with a robe that covers the whole planet)**
- KING: "A SUBJECT! I order you to… eat that flower!" He points at a little weed-flower.
- The sheep is torn. The ghosts whisper "Obey, obey…"
- SHEEP: "No." Silence. The King blinks, then quickly: "…I ORDER you to say no! Excellent obedience!"
- It's funny, and it's the sheep's first real choice.

**7b · THE BUSINESSMAN (grey planet, desk, endless numbers)**
- BUSINESSMAN: "One sheep. I own it. Into the drawer!" He stamps a paper and shoves the box in a drawer.
- In the dark drawer the sheep panics, and **Horns** shows up and head-butts the drawer open: "Nobody puts a sheep in a drawer!" They escape together.
- Horns is embarrassed to be caught being kind.

**7c · THE LAMPLIGHTER (tiny planet, spinning so fast that day and night last one minute)**
- The **LAMPLIGHTER** lights the lamp, puts it out, lights it, puts it out. He hasn't slept in years. "Orders are orders."
- The sheep offers to keep watch so he can sleep for one night. It runs back and forth, lighting and blowing out the lamp, comic and exhausting. It's a long, sweet sequence.
- At dawn the Lamplighter wakes rested and weeps with joy.
- **A golden line fills HALF of the sheep's back gap.** Now the sheep notices. "Is that… me?"

## SEQ 8 · LOST (40:00–45:00)
**EXT. SPACE.** A **storm of stars**: comets, wind and lightning.
- The strings snap one by one. The boy reaches for the box, his fingers touch it, and it slips. "SHEEP!"
- The box tumbles away into the dark. The boy is pulled on by the birds, calling.
- The box crashes onto a **dead planet**: grey rock, cracked, choked with huge old **baobab roots**. It was a planet nobody weeded.
- The sheep crawls out, alone and small. The ghosts appear, quieter now.

SNIFFLE: Maybe this is where unfinished things end up.
HORNS: Like us.

- The sheep sits in the dark with its head down. The lowest point. A long silence.
- Then, very faint and far away, one star glows **gold**. Old Wool squints at it.

OLD WOOL: Mm. That one's looking at you.
SHEEP (standing up): That's his star. I'm going.

# ACT THREE — WHOLE (45:00–60:00)

## SEQ 9 · THE LONG WAY HOME (45:00–49:00)
- The sheep climbs the tallest dead baobab. It's a long, hard climb. The ghosts help: Horns shoves, Sniffle cheers between coughs, and Old Wool naps on a branch.
- At the very top, the sheep uses the box flaps as **wings**. The ghosts blow with all their might.
- It jumps, drifts and glides across the dark toward the gold star.
- It lands, rolling and bumping, on **B612** at dawn, next to the box.

## SEQ 10 · THE ROSE AND THE NIGHT OF SPROUTS (49:00–56:00)
**EXT. B612. DAWN.** It's tiny. Three little volcanoes. A **rose under a glass dome**.

ROSE: Oh! A sheep. Do sheep eat flowers?
SHEEP (starving, honest): …Yes.
ROSE (trembling but proud): I have four thorns. I'm very dangerous.
SHEEP: Then you're safe. Because a good sheep keeps flowers safe.

- The Rose is amazed. They become friends while the sheep waits for the boy, who doesn't come.
- **NIGHT.** The ground cracks: one sprout, then three, then **dozens**. Baobabs. The planet shakes and the rose's dome rattles.
- **The climax.** The sheep runs from sprout to sprout, eating, chewing and crunching. Its tummy is finally full of the right food. The ghosts **join in**, pointing out sprouts and chasing them: Sniffle sneezes a sprout loose, and Horns rams one over.
- Before dawn, **one giant sprout**, already taller than the sheep, roots are about to split the ground under the rose.
- The sheep is exhausted, but it bites, pulls, bites again, and the giant falls.

## SEQ 11 · THE GOLDEN FLASH (56:00–58:30)
- Silence. Dawn. The rose is safe.
- **Gold runs along every unfinished line**: the back, the pencil leg. Flash. The sheep is **whole**.
- The three ghosts glow too. Their pencil lines fill in, and they smile and fade away like warm light. HORNS (almost crying): "Not bad, kid."
- A sound of wings: the **birds** land, and the **boy** jumps off and runs to the rose. He sees her safe, and the sheep beside her, glowing.

BOY: That is exactly the way I wanted it.

- The sheep, whole, presses its head against his hand.

## SEQ 12 · EPILOGUE (58:30–60:00)
**EXT. SAHARA. NIGHT.** The plane is fixed. The **PILOT** (we see his face for the first time, smiling) looks up at the stars before he flies home.
- He takes out his notebook and the drawing of the box, and listens. Laughter, like bells, from the sky.
- One star winks. **We push in**: on it, the boy sleeps, and a whole sheep keeps watch beside a rose.
- **THE END.**

**End credits:** pencil sketches of the adventure (the King, the drawer, the lamp, the ghosts) slowly fill in with colour.

### Story checks
- **What the hero wants:** to be whole. **What it needs:** to learn it is already worth something, and that care is a choice. ✓
- **Gold milestones:** the ear (Seq 4), half the back (Seq 7c), everything (Seq 11). ✓
- **Things set up and paid off:** the baobabs (5 → 10), the rose (5 → 10), the boy's line (2 → 11), the winking star (1 → 12), the ghosts (2 → 11).
- **Positive throughline:** no villain, only fear and neglect, beaten by kindness. ✓
