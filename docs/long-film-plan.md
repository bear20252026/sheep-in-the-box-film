# The Sheep in the Box — Feature Plan (about 60 minutes)

## The one idea that holds the whole film together
A sheep is drawn in a hurry and left **unfinished**: a gap in its back, a leg that is only pencil, an ear left open.
It wants to be **whole**. It learns that it doesn't get finished by being *told* what it is, but by **choosing to look after something**.
Each real act of care finishes one more piece of it, in gold. The last piece comes at the climax.

Every scene must move that idea forward. If a scene doesn't, it gets cut.

- **Hero:** the Sheep. It's curious, hungry, a little scared, and very loyal.
- **The boy** (the little prince) is the sheep's whole world. He made a wish ("draw me a sheep") and the sheep wants to be worth it.
- **The pilot** draws the sheep and wants to get back to fixing his plane. For most of the film he is only his hands and his voice.
- **The three rubbed-out sheep:** these are the drawings the boy said no to (too sickly, a ram with horns, too old). They are erased but still faintly there, like ghosts in pencil. They are funny company, and they are what the hero is afraid of becoming.
- **The rose** on the boy's star is proud, fragile and loved.
- **The baobabs** are the danger. They are tiny sprouts that grow big enough to split a planet.

## ACT ONE — Being drawn (0:00 – 15:00)

| # | Sequence | Time | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The crash** | 0:00–4:00 | Night in the desert. A small plane comes down and the pilot crawls out. At dawn a strange little boy is standing there: "If you please, draw me a sheep." | Sets the world, the boy and the pilot. |
| 2 | **Three wrong sheep** | 4:00–9:00 | The pilot draws a sheep. "No, that one is sick." He draws a second. "That's a ram, it has horns." He draws a third. "Too old." Each one is rubbed out, but each one stays faintly there as a ghost. The pilot is fed up, and he scribbles a sheep fast and stops halfway: our hero. "Wait, I'm not finished!" He draws a box over it: "The sheep you want is inside." | The hero is born unfinished, and the ghosts show what failure looks like. |
| 3 | **Inside the box** | 9:00–15:00 | The sheep wakes in a world of warm cardboard, with light pouring through three holes. The ghost sheep drift in, tease it and scare it. The boy's eye appears at a hole: "That is exactly the way I wanted it!" The sheep makes a vow: "I'll be a very good sheep, and one day I'll be whole." **TITLE.** | What the sheep wants is set, and the promise is made. |

## ACT TWO — The road to his star (15:00 – 45:00)

| # | Sequence | Time | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Night in the desert** | 15:00–21:00 | The boy sleeps. The sheep slips out of the box and gets hungry for the first time. It finds a lonely flower and almost eats it. Then a sandstorm comes, and the sheep shields the flower all night. **The ear is finished in gold.** | First care, first piece of gold. |
| 5 | **The boy's stories** | 21:00–26:00 | By day the pilot fixes the plane while the boy tells stories, drawn in pencil as they float in the air: his tiny star, a rose he loves and doesn't understand, and the baobabs he has to pull up every morning. The sheep listens: "So that's my job." | Plants the rose and the baobab, which pay off in Act Three. |
| 6 | **The birds** | 26:00–31:00 | Wild birds are migrating. The boy ties strings to them, holds the box, and lifts off into the sky. The pilot's hands wave from below. | A big, beautiful set piece that moves the story to a new world. |
| 7 | **Little planets** | 31:00–40:00 | They stop on three small worlds, and each one is a test. **The King** orders the sheep to eat a flower, and the sheep refuses: its first "no". **The Businessman** counts the sheep as "property" and wants to keep it in a drawer, so it escapes. **The Lamplighter** is exhausted from lighting his lamp every minute, and the sheep keeps watch for him for one night. **The back gap is half-filled in gold.** | The sheep learns that care is a choice, not an order. |
| 8 | **Lost** | 40:00–45:00 | A storm between the stars snaps the strings and the box tumbles away. The sheep falls alone onto a dead planet, all rocks and baobab roots. The ghost sheep whisper: "You'll end up like us." The lowest point. Then a faint gold glow in the dark: the boy's star, very far off. | The low point and the reason to go on. |

## ACT THREE — Whole (45:00 – 60:00)

| # | Sequence | Time | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **The long way home** | 45:00–49:00 | The sheep climbs the tallest baobab on the dead planet and jumps, using its wind to drift across. It lands on the boy's star at dawn, alone, next to the box, which landed there first. | The sheep chooses for itself. |
| 10 | **The rose and the night of sprouts** | 49:00–56:00 | The rose is beautiful, and the sheep is starving. It doesn't eat her ("A good sheep keeps flowers safe"). That night the ground cracks: dozens of baobab sprouts, the planet shaking. The sheep eats them one by one, all night. The ghost sheep stop mocking and start helping, pointing out sprouts. At dawn there is one huge last sprout, almost too big for it. | The climax. Everything it learned is put to use. |
| 11 | **The golden flash** | 56:00–58:30 | The last bite. Gold runs along every unfinished line, and the sheep is whole. The ghost sheep are finished too, and they fade away smiling. The boy arrives, carried by the birds, and sees the rose safe: "That is exactly the way I wanted it." | The payoff, and the echo of his line from Act One. |
| 12 | **Epilogue** | 58:30–60:00 | Back on Earth, the pilot looks up at the stars and hears laughter. One star winks. On it, a sheep keeps watch beside a rose. **The End.** | A quiet ending. |

## How we make an hour of it

**Step 1: Lock the story before any animation.**
- First I write the full script with every line.
- Then I build an **animatic** of the whole hour: rough pencil boards with timing, temp sound and subtitles.
- You watch the hour as a moving storyboard and tell me what to change. That's cheap to change now and expensive later.

**Step 2: Make it in 12 sequences.**
- I animate and polish one sequence at a time, in the same look as the short: Remotion and GSAP, grain, light, depth.
- You review each sequence as it's finished, and I re-cut it into the master film each time. The film is always **one** timeline, never stitched parts.

**Step 3: Finish the whole film.**
- Final sound mix, colour grade and credits on the full hour.

**Rough size:** 60 minutes is 86,400 frames, about 20 times the short. Rendering takes about 7 hours for the whole film. The real work is building the animation: plan on **many working sessions**, roughly one sequence (about 5 min of film) per session or two.

## What I need from you

1. **Background paintings.** There are about 25 new places: the crash site, the planets, the storm, the dead planet and so on. Your image tool made the current ones, and new ones from it will match the style best. Otherwise I can paint them in code, but they will look simpler.
2. **Voices.** An hour with subtitles only is hard to watch, so it needs real voices or a text-to-speech tool. You already handle the music, so tell me what you'll use for voices.
3. **Showing the boy.** The short only shows his eye and hands. Over an hour he should be a full character on screen, and I'd design him in the same puppet style as the sheep.
